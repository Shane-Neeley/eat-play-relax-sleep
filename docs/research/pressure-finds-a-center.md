# Pressure Finds a Center

## Research source

The source is the primary research article [Midair Single-Sided Acoustic
Levitation in High-Pressure Regions of Zero-Order Bessel Beams](https://doi.org/10.1103/pfkh-4x7j).
Authors: Yusuke Koroyasu, Christopher Stone, Yoichi Ochiai, Takayuki Hoshi,
Bruce W. Drinkwater, and Tatsuki Fushimi. Published 2026-08-24; accessed
2026-08-29. The publisher marks the article open access under [CC BY
4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Figures, movies, and
third-party components may have separate rights and are not copied here.
It studies acoustic levitation in a high-pressure axial region of a zero-order
Bessel beam, including controlled translation, more than one levitated
particle, and persistence after an obstruction. Those are observations from the
paper's apparatus and conditions, not claims about this EPRS work.

## EPRS translation

EPRS uses the paper as a prompt for an original, renderer-neutral study:

- a stable center becomes a sustained low-frequency anchor;
- controlled translation becomes a slow, independent pan or filter drift;
- an obstruction becomes a brief interruption or spectral occlusion; and
- recovery becomes the return of the anchor without requiring a fixed chorus or
  a predetermined emotional reading.

The resulting music and picture are an original research translation. They are
not a physical simulation, a reconstruction of the apparatus, or evidence that
the audio or video levitates anything. The daily producer may keep, mutate, or
discard this relationship based on the day's prompt and listening/picture
review.
