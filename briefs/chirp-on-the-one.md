# Chirp on the One — production brief

## Promise

Turn a tiny Snowy Tree Cricket field recording into an immediate, bright
126 BPM 4/4 hook: three transformed source-derived chirp events arrive in the
first twelve seconds, then the handclap, bass, and pentatonic melody open into
a sunny pocket.

## Diversity decision

This is intentionally not another dark odd-meter signal piece. Compared with
the recent rattlesnake magnetic-dust and raven twilight-waterline releases,
the song uses familiar 4/4, major pentatonic harmony, daylight gold/green
color, a `meadow` vGPU world, and a `cricket-pulse` motif. The visual is an
authored response with no literal animal character and no animal-language
claim.

## Source and boundary

Snowy Tree Cricket (*Oecanthus fultoni*), iNaturalist observation 393377380,
sound 2129150, recorded by David Anderson, CC BY. The raw source remains
immutable. Three explicit EPRS process recipes create the audible source cuts;
the 4/4 grid is human-authored because the broad source rhythm pass failed its
contrast gate and the successful musical observation only identifies
call-bearing regions and texture evidence.

## Release combo

Source-derived audio + 4/4 dance pocket + A major pentatonic + headless vGPU
meadow + one-minute YouTube cut + captions/chapters + complete attribution.
